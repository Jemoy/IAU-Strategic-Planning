# Aviation Ground Instructor FGD – Consolidated Problem Analysis

*Sources: Ground Instructor FGD Survey (20 respondents, July 16–21, 2026); Minutes of the Aviation Ground Instructor Meeting (July 16, 2026); Department problem-analysis document. Indiana Aerospace University – School of Aviation and Engineering.*

## I. Problems, Connected Problems, Sources, and Impact

| # | Primary Problem | Connected Problems | Source(s) | Impact on the Department |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Curriculum not aligned with CAAP operational requirements** | • Pilot Ground Course distribution across 1st and 2nd year<br>• BAF/IFR sequencing (IFR moved to 3rd year while sim schedules remain difficult)<br>• FOO elective at ~170 hours vs. CAAP requirement of 400 hours + 90-day OJT<br>• Theory taught out of sync with when students apply it in flight training<br>• Only four weeks remaining to finalize curriculum revisions | Minutes (Sec. I, II); Survey – 9 respondents | Students complete academic subjects but cannot complete operational requirements; conflicts with both CHED and CAAP; delayed graduation and licensing. |
| 2 | **No integrated simulator and flight scheduling system** | • Long waits for sim/flight slots even after fees are paid<br>• Simulation concentrated in Semi-Final and Final periods<br>• Delayed simulation grades and missed university deadlines<br>• One-year window to complete sim requirements breeds complacency<br>• Too few simulators for the number of students | Minutes (Sec. II, IV); Survey – 13 respondents (most-cited issue) | Delayed completion, student dissatisfaction, transfers to other institutions, and instructor workload spikes at semester end. |
| 3 | **Weak prerequisite enforcement** | • Students bypass prerequisite simulator subjects through administrative processing and payment arrangements<br>• No clear or enforced flight-training milestones per year level | Minutes (Sec. II, IV); Survey | Competency gaps during advanced training; inconsistent academic and flight progression. |
| 4 | **Aviation Admin processing and documentation delays** | • Delays in licenses, student pilot permits, check rides, and renewals<br>• Poor record-keeping and archiving; no document-tracking system<br>• Repeated online complaints about how students are treated by the admin office | Survey – 6 respondents; Minutes (Sec. IV) | Blocks training progression; long-standing reputational damage; every aviation student passes through this office. |
| 5 | **Poor transparency and communication** | • Recurring student concern: "Dako kay mig bayad pero wa mi nakitan"<br>• Simulator and flight schedules not published to students<br>• No pre-deployment briefings for each flight-training phase<br>• Information beneficial to instructors not disseminated<br>• Negative online reviews affecting public image | Minutes (Sec. I, IV); Survey | Mistrust, low morale, student transfers, and poor institutional reputation. |
| 6 | **Instructor competency gaps and lack of standardization** | • Newly hired/fresh-graduate GIs lack teaching experience and operational exposure<br>• Difficulty answering technical questions from students<br>• Completion of GI subjects does not qualify one as a licensed GI; many GIs unlicensed<br>• No standardized PowerPoints/visual aids per subject<br>• No instructor handbook incorporating PCAR and company rules<br>• Only four FSIs are qualified to teach and sign simulator grades | Minutes (Sec. I, III); Survey – 5 respondents | Reduced instructional quality and student confidence; simulator bottleneck; compliance risk with industry expectations. |
| 7 | **Limited faculty development and unclear career progression** | • Recommendation-based FI selection; few instructors qualify<br>• No clear, non-seniority-based path to FSI/FI positions<br>• Instructors shoulder the cost of their own ratings and proficiency checks<br>• Little support for graduate studies and research<br>• Uneven workload distribution<br>• Proposed curriculum may reduce GI teaching loads | Minutes (Sec. I, III, IV); Survey – 10 respondents | Stalled careers, weak loyalty and retention, and limited long-term departmental capability. |
| 8 | **Uncompetitive salary and welfare** | • No salary increase in roughly 10 years<br>• No performance incentives or competitive benefits<br>• Request for a Lead Pilot/Admin psychological assessment | Survey – 5 respondents | Low morale; difficulty retaining and attracting qualified instructors. |
| 9 | **Inadequate facilities, aircraft, and equipment** | • Classrooms described as gloomy and not premium (no tiles)<br>• Aircraft parts shortages and AMO/maintenance delays<br>• Flight simulator system needs upgrading<br>• Required instructional materials and facilities for some subjects are absent | Survey – 6 respondents; Minutes (Sec. I) | Undermines the premium training experience students pay for; slows flight training progress. |
| 10 | **No departmental Operational Plan** | • Budgeting, projects, faculty development, and conferences handled independently<br>• No SMS/QMS culture reinforced across academics and flight operations<br>• Too few meetings for curriculum review and training<br>• No operational plan comparable to the Aerospace Engineering Department's | Minutes (Sec. I, IV); Survey – 4 respondents | Reactive rather than strategic decision-making; no accountability, KPIs, or clear priorities. |
| 11 | **Weak industry integration** | • Limited partnerships with airlines, cargo operators, private aviation companies, and cadet programs<br>• Few professional certifications and industry exposure for students | Minutes (Sec. IV); Survey – 4 respondents | Fewer employment pathways for graduates; weaker industry readiness. |

## II. Solutions and Actions

*Items marked "Proposed solution" (Problems 9 and 11) are recommendations for team review; all other items reflect actions already taken or in progress as of September 8, 2026.*

| Prob. # | Primary Problem | Solution / Action | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Curriculum not aligned with CAAP operational requirements** | a. Curriculum restructured by grouping CAAP-required courses into single subject courses. All PPL lectures are now in 1st Year 1st Semester, CPL in 3rd Year, IR in 3rd Year, and Multi-Engine in 4th Year. This resolves the out-of-sync training.<br>b. FOO (CAAP 400 hours) – Temporary: students' flying hours are credited as their OJT. Full solution: the new curriculum adds a dedicated OJT subject course that satisfies CAAP's OJT requirements.<br>c. The updated curriculum has been submitted to the administration. | Submitted to Administration – September 8, 2026 |
| 2, 4 | **No integrated scheduling system; Aviation Admin processing and documentation delays** | a. A dedicated team is being formed to build the flying school scheduling system.<br>b. The integrated system will focus on transparency and on eliminating the bypass approach used by students. | Team being formed |
| 3 | **Weak prerequisite enforcement** | a. Temporary: the Aviation Admin is checking prerequisites for each student one by one.<br>b. Full solution: coordinate with the IT Team for complete system integration. | Manual checking ongoing; IT coordination pending |
| 5 | **Poor transparency and communication** | a. The team is building a strategic approach to address the negative reviews received from students, protect the image of the school, and increase morale. | In development |
| 6, 7 | **Instructor competency gaps; limited faculty development and unclear career progression** | a. More faculty development to be provided to the GIs, including additional trainings; GIs will be asked what kind of trainings they need.<br>b. FI selection and standardization process is currently under review to determine the correct steps.<br>c. Faculty graduate studies support to be proposed for Academic Year 2027–2028.<br>d. Possible reduction in GI loads under the proposed curriculum is being addressed by adding new subjects such as UAS and SMS. | Ongoing; graduate studies proposal for AY 2027–2028 |
| 8 | **Uncompetitive salary and welfare** | a. The concern will be raised with the administration. | For administration review |
| 9 | **Inadequate facilities, aircraft, and equipment** | *Proposed solution:*<br>a. Conduct a facilities and equipment audit per subject (classrooms, simulators, aircraft, instructional materials) and rank gaps by impact on CAAP compliance and student progression.<br>b. Include a prioritized facilities budget line in the Department Operational Plan, starting with classroom refurbishment and a spare-parts inventory for the training fleet.<br>c. Establish a preventive maintenance schedule for simulators and aircraft with the AMO, and track equipment downtime in the integrated scheduling system so lost training hours are visible.<br>d. Explore industry sponsorship or donated equipment through the partnerships developed under Problem 11. | Proposed – for team review |
| 10 | **No departmental Operational Plan** | a. The team is currently building the IAU Strategic Plan, which includes each department's operational plan. | In development |
| 11 | **Weak industry integration** | *Proposed solution:*<br>a. Designate an industry-linkages lead and target formal MOAs with 3–5 partners (airlines, cargo operators, charter/private operators, cadet programs) within AY 2026–2027.<br>b. Structure the new OJT subject course so it can be served with partner operators, giving students industry exposure while satisfying CAAP's 90-day OJT requirement.<br>c. Hold an annual Aviation Career Fair / industry day and invite partner recruiters and CAAP representatives.<br>d. Build an alumni network of IAU graduates now in airlines to mentor students and open cadet-program referrals. | Proposed – for team review |
